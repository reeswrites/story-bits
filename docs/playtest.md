# Playtest: Story Bits with friends

20 minutes, 3–6 people on phones, in a room or on a call. Goal: find out whether this is fun with friends before building more. Run it with **Small Poems**; it needs no jokes to land, so nobody feels tested.

## Before (5 minutes, you alone)

- Open reeswrites.com/story-bits on your own phone. Pick **Small Poems**. Roll once to check it works.
- **Start a jam** from that page. The jam bar gives three links:
  - **edit link**: everyone who plays gets this one.
  - **comment link**: for anyone who'd rather react than write.
  - **view link**: for a late arrival, or someone watching.
- Paste the **edit link** into the group chat now, with one line: "tap this, don't do anything yet." Don't explain the rules in the message.
- Roles: you are **host** (you start rounds and reveals). Everyone else is a **writer**. With six people, one can be the **scribe**: they note what you'd miss (see Notes below).
- Have a second link ready for the pass-it-around round (you make it then, not now).
- Phones: charge, volume off, rotate lock off. Wi-Fi or data: ask who has weak signal before you start.

## Run sheet

| Min | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Intro | Say one thing: "You'll build tiny poems out of cards. Nothing has to make sense." Don't demo. |
| 2–5 | Solo warm-up | Everyone opens the **edit link** and plays alone, in their own tab or copy: roll, tap a word to swap it, slide the dial from **Literal** to **Surreal**. Ask each person to find one line they like and read it out when time is up. Round-robin readout. |
| 5–10 | Together jam | Everyone is in one shared piece. Rule: **one stanza, one piece, everyone edits at once.** Pick the "With a coda" length. Goal: finish a poem you'd all read out loud. You do not steer; say "keep going" and "stop at 5 minutes". Try the dial at **Figurative** first, then push to **Surreal** together. Read the result out loud together. |
| 10–13 | Workshop, no claps | Switch to **Workshop**, no move limit, claps off. Everyone starts from the same piece and rewrites their own copy for 2 minutes. Reveal. Each person leaves one comment on someone else's copy. No scoring. |
| 13–16 | Workshop, with claps | Same again with a **move limit of 5** and **claps on**. New starting piece. Reveal, then clap. Say winners out loud; don't make it a big deal. |
| 16–18 | Pass it around | Hit **Pass it around**, send the link, everyone makes a version and sends back a link. Open them side by side. Skip if time is short (see "If you run long"). |
| 18–20 | Questions | The six questions below, quickly, out loud, one at a time. Scribe writes the answers. |

Try one constraint if there is slack: fork Small Poems, set **no letter "e"**, play one round. Watch whether people enjoy the squeeze or give up on it.

## What to watch for

**Where people get stuck**
- Who taps a card but doesn't understand what "tune" means. Note when they stop and what they say.
- Who never changes a word and just watches.
- Who can't tell which link is which (edit vs comment vs view).
- The dial: does anyone find it unprompted?
- Moves when a limit is on: do people notice they're running out, or does it surprise them?

**What makes people laugh or go "oh"**
- Write down the exact line, the word, and who said something out loud. The line itself matters more than the reaction.
- Note whether it's the clash on the **Surreal** setting or a perfectly plausible line.
- Note moments where someone reads a line out unprompted.

**Phone and network**
- Slow or missing updates in the together jam. Who sees what, and how late.
- A phone whose screen wraps badly, or buttons too small to tap.
- Anyone dropped from the jam and having to reopen the link.
- Whether claps and comments land for everyone at the reveal.
- The on-screen keyboard blocking something.

## Questions to ask after

1. Which moment was the most fun? Which was the most boring?
2. Was there a point you didn't know what to do next? What was it?
3. Together or workshop: which would you do again with a different group, and why?
4. Did the move limit and claps make it better, worse, or not matter?
5. Did the poems feel like yours, or like the machine's? What would change that?
6. Would you open a link like this on your own? What would you do with it?
7. (Optional) What would you want it to make: a longer poem, a game, something to post?

## If you run long

Cut in this order: the constraint round, then pass-it-around, then the second workshop. Never cut the together jam or the questions.
